Common DAO: Council, one voting rule, and a wallet per DAO

An explainer for gnolang/gno PR 6012. Page generated by claude-opus-5.

A Common DAO is an on-chain group that decides things by vote. Before this change, a DAO tracked its members through a layer of storage and grouping machinery, counted votes against every member it had, held no money of its own, and had no address. This change removes that machinery and replaces it with three simpler ideas: a flat list of voting members, one fixed rule for counting votes, and a wallet that belongs to the DAO and can only be spent by a proposal that passed.

DAOs form a tree. A DAO can create sub-DAOs, and an ancestor keeps a measure of control over its descendants: it can pull a descendant's money back up the tree, freeze a descendant's wallet, change a descendant's member list, and dissolve it. That tree, and where money is allowed to travel inside it, is the part of this change with the most moving pieces.

Concepts

Council. The list of addresses allowed to vote in a DAO. It is a plain set of addresses. It changes only through a proposal that passed, or when a member resigns.

Electorate. A photograph of the Council taken the moment a proposal is created. That photograph, not the live Council, decides who may vote on that proposal. Someone added to the Council afterwards votes on the next proposal instead. Someone removed afterwards stays in the photograph, and their silence still counts.

Denominator. The number the vote count is measured against. It is the size of the electorate minus the number of abstentions. Abstaining shrinks it, which is a way of saying "decide without me". Not voting at all does not shrink it, which is a way of saying silence counts as opposition.

Threshold. Two settings, fixed per kind of proposal, not chosen by the proposer. Supermajority needs two thirds of the denominator or more. Simple majority needs more than half. Either way, a majority of NO votes dismisses the proposal.

Treasury. Each DAO gets its own address, computed from the realm's own address plus the DAO's number. Nobody holds a key to it. The only code that can move money out of it is a proposal executor, and only after the proposal passed.

Two things the calculator makes visible

Nothing passes on zero YES votes, because two thirds of a positive denominator is always more than zero. And a single YES can carry a proposal in a large group, as long as enough of the others abstain: each abstention shrinks the denominator the YES is measured against.

Who decides what

The threshold is a property of the kind of proposal, so a proposer cannot pick an easier bar.

Where money is allowed to move

Two rules hold the tree together. Money entering a DAO's wallet can leave only through a passed proposal, and the ancestor powers that reach down into a descendant always send money one step up the tree rather than out of it. So a parent can recall what it funded, and a stranger cannot be handed a descendant's balance by an ancestor vote.

                 Root DAO
                 wallet: derived from realm address + "dao/1"
                   |
      spend  ------+------  any address, needs the Root's own supermajority
                   |
                 Sub DAO
                 wallet: derived from realm address + "dao/2"
                   |
      clawback ----+  Root votes, balance moves to the Sub's parent
      freeze   ----+  Root votes, the Sub can no longer spend
      dissolve ----+  Root votes, balance sweeps, the Sub retires
                   |
                 Sub-sub DAO

Freezing does not cascade: an ancestor freezes each descendant it wants frozen. A frozen DAO cannot unfreeze itself while any living ancestor remains, because the ancestors are the ones who hold that power. There is one exception. If every ancestor above a frozen DAO has been dissolved, nobody is left who could ever unfreeze it, so the frozen DAO's own Council is allowed to unfreeze itself. Without that exception, dissolving the chain above a frozen DAO would lock its money away permanently.

How a DAO spends without holding a key

The wallet address is not a keypair. It is derived from the realm's package path and the DAO's number, so anyone can compute it without asking the realm, and the same DAO gets the same address across a redeploy at the same path. The realm refuses to start if it is deployed anywhere else, because the address it computes and the identity it can actually act as would then disagree.

When a proposal executes, the realm mints a narrow identity for exactly one DAO and hands it to the executor. The bank refuses to send from any address other than that identity's own. So an executor holding the identity for DAO 7 can move DAO 7's coins and nothing else, no matter what else it tries.

Lifecycle

A proposal is re-counted after every vote and decided the moment the count says so, which can be well before its deadline. A proposal still undecided when its deadline passes is dismissed rather than left open. Once the deadline has passed anyone may finalize it, because the count is fixed and finalizing it is not a choice. Before the deadline, only a Council member may push a proposal that already passed through to execution.

StateMeaning
activeAccepting votes.
passedThe count settled in favour. Waiting to be executed.
dismissedVoted down, or still undecided at the deadline.
withdrawnPulled by its creator before anyone voted.
executedRan successfully.
failedPassed, but its preconditions no longer held when it ran.

Dissolving a DAO dismisses everything it still had in flight, so nothing is left pending on a DAO that no longer answers. Dissolution cannot be undone.

Keeping the internals in

Everything a DAO owns is unexported, and anything handed across a realm boundary is a read-only view. The view for a proposal deliberately flattens its contents into a title and a body rather than exposing the proposal's definition, because the definition carries the executor, and a caller holding the executor could run it with the hosting realm's authority.
